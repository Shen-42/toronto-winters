Were Toronto Winters as Snowy as I Remember as a Kid?

I recently stumbled on Volodymyr Agafonkin's Observable notebook asking whether Kyiv winters were as snowy as he remembered. It got me thinking about my own childhood in Toronto, tobogganing in the parks with my brothers. I kind of miss how snowy it used to be. So I wanted to check whether Toronto winters really were snowier back then, or whether I just remember them that way.

Observations

  • 2013–14 was the snowiest winter in the record. Snow covered the ground almost without a break from late November to the end of March, including 109 days in a row. It was also the coldest winter, averaging −5.5°C from December to February.
  • 2011–12 had almost no snow: only 23 days with snow on the ground. It was also one of the warmest winters in the record.
  • 2008–09 had a white Christmas, with 16 cm of snow on the ground on December 25.
  • 2024–25 came after my childhood, but it had the deepest snow in the record: 46 cm on February 17, 2025.
  • In recent years, especially in high school, it felt like Toronto got little to no snow. The data partly backs this up. 2023–24 had only 26 days with snow on the ground and was the warmest winter in the record. But other recent winters, like 2019–20 and 2021–22, had plenty of snow.

So, were winters snowier when I was a kid?

Over the whole record, Toronto winters warmed by about 1°C per decade and lost about 5 days of snow cover per decade. Nineteen winters is a short record, though, and one unusual year moves those trends a lot. The years with deep, lasting snow that I remember, like 2013–14, were real, but they were also unusual.

In the two winters both stations recorded, North York had 17 more days of snow cover in 2012–13 and 4 fewer in 2013–14. The downtown station also leaves many single days unrecorded, so some winters may have had more snow cover than the chart shows. Temperatures are from the downtown station.
